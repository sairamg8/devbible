# Link map · System design · Part 1 (phases 0–1)

Covers every topic row of phases 0 and 1 in `docs/system-design/syllabus/01-the-interview-and-the-method.md`: what design interviews test (T00-01 to T00-13) and the method from requirements to deep dives (T01-01 to T01-18). That is 31 rows. All of them have a written page of ours (`docs/system-design/pages` holds phases 0 to 3), so every block is marked written and its delta describes what our page or row says that the sources do not. Two rows run over two pages: T00-01 continues in `01b-staff-and-reading-the-room.md` and T01-11 in `11b-the-load-walk-and-the-price-of-redundancy.md`.
*read* = I read the passage that supports the claim on the fetched page; *headings only* = fetched at HTTP 200 and the title or headings checked, passage not read for the claim. Third-party links carry a date only where the page shows one. No `#anchors` are given. Where no good non-official explanation turned up, the block has no "Beyond the docs" line. Official means the Google SRE book, vendor and standards documentation, the papers' own pages and the authors' own writing; interview-prep sites, blogs and courses count as beyond the docs, even when one is the best "Read this". AWS Builders' Library addresses redirect to builder.aws.com, so the final address is given.

### T00-04 · The vocabulary contract — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-0-the-interview/04-the-vocabulary-contract.md`
- **Read this:** [Please stop calling databases CP or AP](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/05/11/please-stop-calling-databases-cp-or-ap.html) · Martin Kleppmann, 2015 · in the CAP proof "consistency" means linearizability, unrelated to the C in ACID, and "availability" means every non-failing node answers; the labels CP and AP hide real differences (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — consistency and availability only, not latency versus throughput, scalability versus performance or reliability versus resilience · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Service Level Objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/) · Google SRE book · defines SLI, SLO and SLA, and prefers percentiles over averages because an average hides a long slow tail (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [PACELC theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PACELC_theorem) · Wikipedia · Daniel Abadi's extension of CAP: under a partition choose availability or consistency, else choose latency or consistency (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Beyond the docs:** [System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer) · Donne Martin · short paired sections on performance versus scalability (slow for one user versus slow only under load), latency versus throughput, and availability versus consistency (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** five paired terms with the one-line test for each that stops loose use, in the interview register rather than the theorem's.

### T00-05 · The latency ladder — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-0-the-interview/05-the-latency-ladder.md`
- **Read this:** [Latency numbers every programmer should know](https://gist.github.com/jboner/2841832) · gist by Jonas Bonér, figures credited to Jeff Dean and originally Peter Norvig · labelled about 2012: L1 reference 0.5 ns, main memory 100 ns, random 4K SSD read 150 microseconds, same-datacentre round trip 500 microseconds, disk seek 10 ms, California to Netherlands and back 150 ms (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — the raw numbers are dated and given without the habit of reaching for them · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Latency numbers, interactive](https://colin-scott.github.io/personal_website/research/interactive_latency.html) · Colin Scott · the same ladder with a year slider; values are extrapolated from trend formulas, while the datacentre and cross-continent round trips are held constant (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Response times: the 3 important limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) · Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group, 1993 · 0.1 s feels instantaneous, 1 s keeps the flow of thought, 10 s is the limit of attention; the thresholds that turn a latency budget into a target (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Beyond the docs:** [Numbers everyone should know](https://highscalability.com/numbers-everyone-should-know/) · Todd Hoff, High Scalability, 2009-02-18 · Jeff Dean's figures used for capacity arithmetic, such as about 100 disk seeks a second and the fetch rate implied by reading 1 MB from memory (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** the ladder as orders of magnitude only, six ratios that decide designs, what has changed since the table was written, and the practice of quoting a rung aloud when justifying a cache or a region choice.

## Phase 1 — The method: requirements to deep dives

### T01-01 · Functional requirements — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/01-functional-requirements.md`
- **Read this:** [System Design in a Hurry: Delivery](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/delivery) · Hello Interview · requirements come first, about 5 minutes, functional then prioritised non-functional (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — no explicit out-of-scope list or actor list · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [A framework for system design interviews](https://bytebytego.com/courses/system-design-interview/a-framework-for-system-design-interviews) · ByteByteGo · step one is establishing scope with clarifying questions, and any assumption the interviewer asks you to make is written down (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** three to five journeys, an explicit out-of-scope list and named actors written on the board so the interviewer can redirect before design.

### T01-02 · Non-functional requirements — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/02-non-functional-requirements.md`
- **Read this:** [Service Level Objectives](https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/) · Google SRE book · SLIs as measured indicators, SLOs as targets on them, and percentiles preferred because an average can hide a slow tail (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — targets and measurement, not per-journey consistency, retention or compliance · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Embracing Risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/) · Google SRE book · the availability target is a floor and a ceiling, each extra increment can cost 100 times the last, and availability can be measured by time or by the share of successful requests (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** scale, p50 and p99, availability, per-journey consistency, durability, retention and compliance as a checklist where each skipped item is a question you will be asked.

### T01-03 · Back-of-the-envelope estimation — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/03-back-of-the-envelope-estimation.md`
- **Read this:** [Back-of-the-envelope estimation](https://bytebytego.com/courses/system-design-interview/back-of-the-envelope-estimation) · ByteByteGo, from Alex Xu's book · powers of two as thousand, million, billion, round numbers, write assumptions down, label units, and a worked example giving about 3,500 posts per second, a peak of about 7,000 and 30 TB per day of media (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — the arithmetic is there and cache size is named as one to practise, but not cache sizing from a hot fraction worked through or saying aloud that you are rounding · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Little's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little%27s_law) · Wikipedia · concurrency equals arrival rate times time in system, useful for turning QPS into connections or workers (*headings only*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** QPS, storage, bandwidth and cache size as four formulas, with the habit of stating the rounding and what each number licenses.

### T01-04 · Traffic shapes — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/04-traffic-shapes.md`
- **Read this:** [Using load shedding to avoid overload](https://builder.aws.com/content/3Eun1EEyX6p2e3VYNyRLSJzLuMV/using-load-shedding-to-avoid-overload) · Amazon Builders' Library · overload feeds itself through timeouts and retries, so a server sheds excess requests early to keep goodput up; shedding is one layer next to autoscaling and headroom for losing a zone, not a replacement for them (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — the shedding side, not steady versus diurnal versus spike shapes or the peak-to-average ratio · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Handling Overload](https://sre.google/sre-book/handling-overload/) · Google SRE book · per-customer limits, client-side throttling, four criticality levels that decide what is rejected first, and retry budgets (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** the three shapes, the peak-to-average ratio as the number to say, and the difference between designing for the peak and shedding at it.

### T01-05 · The API sketch — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/05-the-api-sketch.md`
- **Read this:** [Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) · Stripe API reference · for API v1, the client sends an Idempotency-Key of up to 255 characters, the first response (500 errors included) is saved once execution begins and replayed on retries, keys may be pruned after 24 hours, and reusing a key with different parameters is an error (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — idempotency only, not pagination or how a wrong API leaks into later boxes · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [AIP-158: Pagination](https://google.aip.dev/158) · Google API Improvement Proposals · page size and opaque page tokens, and pagination added from the start because adding it later is a backwards-incompatible change (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [API Design Guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/apis/design) · Google Cloud · resource-oriented design, resource names, standard and custom methods (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Beyond the docs:** [Implementing Stripe-like idempotency keys in Postgres](https://brandur.org/idempotency-keys) · Brandur Leach, 2017-10-27 · an idempotency-key table with recovery points and atomic phases committed around each foreign call, the server side of the key on our own stack (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** one endpoint per journey, idempotency on anything that charges or creates, pagination on anything that lists, and the cost of a wrong API downstream.

### T01-06 · The data model from the access patterns — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/06-the-data-model-from-access-patterns.md`
- **Read this:** [System Design in a Hurry: Delivery](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/delivery) · Hello Interview · core entities as a short bulleted first draft, about 2 minutes, before the API and design, extended as more are discovered (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — entities first, not access patterns first or choosing the store from them · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Designing Data-Intensive Applications](https://dataintensive.net/) · Martin Kleppmann · the chapters on data models and storage engines tie the model to how it is queried (*headings only*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Beyond the docs:** [The what, why, and when of single-table design with DynamoDB](https://www.alexdebrie.com/posts/dynamodb-single-table/) · Alex DeBrie, 2020-02-05 · the strongest statement of "start with your access patterns first", written down before any item is laid out (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** keys and relationships written after listing how each journey reads and writes, so the primary store comes from the patterns and not from habit.

### T01-07 · The high-level diagram — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/07-the-high-level-diagram.md`
- **Read this:** [System Design in a Hurry: Delivery](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/delivery) · Hello Interview · high-level design takes 10 to 15 minutes, built one endpoint at a time (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — no numbered flows or fixed box vocabulary · fetched 2026-10-14

### T01-08 · Read path and write path, separately — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/08-read-path-and-write-path.md`
- **Read this:** [Eventually Consistent](https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/2008/12/eventually_consistent.html) · Werner Vogels, 2008 · with N replicas, W write acknowledgements and R read replicas, W plus R greater than N makes the sets overlap; R of 1 optimises reads and W of 1 optimises writes, and client guarantees such as read-your-writes and monotonic reads are defined (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — replica-level consistency, not caches and fan-out versus durability and ordering · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Designing Data-Intensive Applications](https://dataintensive.net/) · Martin Kleppmann · replication and derived data cover the two paths in depth (*headings only*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** the walk of "the user taps Buy" end to end, box by box, with the read side and write side kept apart.

### T01-09 · Choosing the deep dives — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/09-choosing-the-deep-dives.md`

- **Our delta:** three selection tests: hardest scale, where the interviewer keeps returning, and what you can defend under three follow-ups.

### T01-11 · Bottlenecks and single points of failure — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-1-the-method/11-bottlenecks-and-single-points-of-failure.md`
- **Read this:** [Addressing Cascading Failures](https://sre.google/sre-book/addressing-cascading-failures/) · Google SRE book · a failure that grows through positive feedback; when one cluster fails its load shifts to the next, which overloads and fails in turn (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — failure propagation, not the SPOF checklist · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Static stability using Availability Zones](https://builder.aws.com/content/3K9SiqiJ369TGkkGzqPM95BuYWI/static-stability-using-availability-zones) · Amazon Builders' Library · a statically stable system keeps working when a dependency is impaired, by being provisioned for the zone loss beforehand rather than reacting to it (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** the walk "what if this box dies", with a SPOF checklist of the classic five (DNS, balancer, primary database, the one queue, the one region) and five that walk past (cache, single consumer, a secret or certificate, the config store, the deploy itself); the continuation page carries "what if this number doubles" and the price of redundancy.
