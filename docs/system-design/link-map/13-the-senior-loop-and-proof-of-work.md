# Link map · System design · Part 13 (phase 23)

Covers every topic row of phase 23 in `docs/system-design/syllabus/13-the-senior-loop-and-proof-of-work.md`: the senior loop, proof of work and the plan (T23-01 to T23-18). That is 18 rows. Five have a written page of ours (all in phase 0: the rounds, levelling, mocks, reading the room and remote tooling); the phase 0 practice page gives an eight-week design-only plan and defers the twelve-week plan to this part, so that row is marked not written. `docs/system-design/pages` holds phases 0 to 3 only, so the other thirteen are marked not written and their delta describes what the syllabus row promises.
*read* = I read the passage that supports the claim on the fetched page; *headings only* = fetched at HTTP 200 and the title or headings checked, passage not read for the claim. Third-party links carry a date only where the page shows one. No `#anchors` are given. This part is about hiring practice, so there is little official documentation: most sources are company hiring pages, project documentation and practitioner blogs, and the block says which. Where no good non-official explanation turned up, the block has no "Beyond the docs" line.

### T23-01 · How product-company loops run — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-0-the-interview/02-the-three-design-rounds.md`
- **Read this:** [Understand the interview process for software engineers](https://formation.dev/blog/understand-the-interview-process-for-software-engineers) · Formation · seven stages: application review, a 20 to 30 minute recruiter screen, a technical screen or online assessment, the full technical and behavioural loop with system design weighted far more for senior and staff roles, the on-site, a hiring committee that reads the packet to calibrate against the bar and decide whether it supports a hire at the target level, then offer, background check and references; startups often compress or skip the committee (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — the stages and who decides, not what each round is for in a design track · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) · Amazon · the list of principles that an Amazon behavioural round is scored against; the page does not describe the bar-raiser role (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — one company's behavioural axis only · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** which round grades which skill, why a loop runs HLD, LLD and machine coding as three separate rounds, and confirming the round before it starts. Our page covers the three design rounds only; the screen, DSA, hiring-manager and behavioural or bar-raiser rounds, who decides and where the level is set are still owed by this part.

### T23-03 · The behavioural round — Master · 🔲 not written
- **Read this:** [Behavioral interview](https://techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/) · Tech Interview Handbook · the round checks for a history of the behaviours that succeed at the company and gauges seniority; collect 3 to 5 projects of high impact, complexity and personal involvement, tell each as a STAR story, add a Reflection step for seniority, write bullets rather than memorised scripts and rehearse aloud (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — a smaller story bank than six, and no reason seniors fail this round more than design · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Situation, task, action, result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situation,_task,_action,_result) · Wikipedia · the STAR technique for behavioural questions; the action covers what you did, why and the alternatives, the result covers the outcome and what you learned (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) · Amazon · the sixteen principles, such as Ownership, Dive Deep and Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit; the page lists them but does not describe the interview or how stories are scored (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** the syllabus row promises six named stories (ownership, conflict, failure, influence without authority, a hard trade-off, measurable impact) and the argument that seniors lose this round more often than the design one.

### T23-04 · "Tell me about a system you owned" — Master · 🔲 not written
- **Read this:** [Behavioral interview](https://techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/) · Tech Interview Handbook · lists "tell me about your most impactful project" as a common question, says to pick a project at the intersection of impact, scope and personal involvement, and in the action step the interviewer wants what you did, why and what the alternatives were, closing with what you learned (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — the shape of an answer, not numbers or what you would change in a system · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Design Docs at Google](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/) · Malte Ubl · a design doc records the high-level strategy and key decisions with the trade-offs behind them, with context, goals and non-goals, and alternatives considered as one of the most important sections; the same structure is a template for narrating a system you owned (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — a writing format, not a spoken answer · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** the syllabus row promises context, decisions, trade-offs, numbers and what you would change, and the contrast between an answer that shows ownership and one that lists technologies.

### T23-05 · Proof of work — Master · 🔲 not written

### T23-09 · Mock interviews — Understand · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-0-the-interview/10-how-to-practise.md`
