# Link map · System design · Part 13 (phase 23)

Covers every topic row of phase 23 in `docs/system-design/syllabus/13-the-senior-loop-and-proof-of-work.md`: the senior loop, proof of work and the plan (T23-01 to T23-18). That is 18 rows. Six have a written page of ours (all in phase 0: the rounds, levelling, the practice plan, mocks, reading the room and remote tooling); `docs/system-design/pages` holds phases 0 to 3 only, so the other twelve are marked not written and their delta describes what the syllabus row promises.
*read* = I read the passage that supports the claim on the fetched page; *headings only* = fetched at HTTP 200 and the title or headings checked, passage not read for the claim. Third-party links carry a date only where the page shows one. No `#anchors` are given. This part is about hiring practice, so there is little official documentation: most sources are company hiring pages, project documentation and practitioner blogs, and the block says which. Where no good non-official explanation turned up, the block has no "Beyond the docs" line.

## Phase 23 — The senior loop, proof of work and the plan

### T23-01 · How product-company loops run — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-0-the-interview/02-the-three-design-rounds.md`
- **Read this:** [Understand the interview process for software engineers](https://formation.dev/blog/understand-the-interview-process-for-software-engineers/) · Formation · seven stages: application review, a 20 to 30 minute recruiter screen, a technical screen, the full technical and behavioural loop with system design weighted more for senior roles, the on-site, a hiring committee that reads written feedback to calibrate and decide hire, different level or no-hire, then offer and references; startups often skip the committee (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — the stages and who decides, not what each round is for in a design track · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) · Amazon · the list of principles that an Amazon behavioural round is scored against; the page does not describe the bar-raiser role (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — one company's behavioural axis only · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** which round grades which skill, why a loop runs HLD, LLD and machine coding as three separate rounds, and confirming the round before it starts.

### T23-02 · Levelling — Master · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-0-the-interview/01-what-design-means-at-each-level.md`

- **Our delta:** one question (the storefront checkout) answered as SDE-2, senior and staff, so the level is visible in the answer rather than in a job title.

### T23-03 · The behavioural round — Master · 🔲 not written
- **Read this:** [Behavioral interview](https://techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/) · Tech Interview Handbook · interviewers judge track record of working with others, seniority, values fit and soft skills; pick 3 to 5 high-impact projects, tell them in STAR, add a reflection step, write bullets rather than scripts and rehearse aloud (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — a smaller story bank than six, and no reason seniors fail this round more than design · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [STAR format](https://techinterviewhandbook.org/star-format/) · Tech Interview Handbook · Situation, Task, Action, Result with the stress on action and the alternatives you considered, plus a Reflection step for seniority (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) · Amazon · the principles stories are mapped to, such as Ownership, Dive Deep and Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit (*read*) · fetched 2026-10-14

### T23-04 · "Tell me about a system you owned" — Master · 🔲 not written

- **Also good:** [Design Docs at Google](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/) · Malte Ubl · a design doc is about decisions and trade-offs, with goals, non-goals and alternatives considered as the sections that matter; the same structure is a template for narrating a system you owned (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — a writing format, not a spoken answer · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Our delta:** the syllabus row promises context, decisions, trade-offs, numbers and what you would change, and the contrast between an answer that shows ownership and one that lists technologies.

### T23-05 · Proof of work — Master · 🔲 not written

### T23-09 · Mock interviews — Understand · ✅ written `docs/system-design/pages/phase-0-the-interview/10-how-to-practise.md`

### T23-14 · Writing as a senior skill — Understand · 🔲 not written
- **Read this:** [Design Docs at Google](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/) · Malte Ubl · design docs record the high-level approach and key decisions with emphasis on trade-offs; alternatives considered is one of the most important sections; length from a 1 to 3 page mini doc to 10 to 20 pages; do not write one when the solution is unambiguous (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — design docs, not postmortems or review comments · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [Postmortem culture](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/) · Google SRE book · blameless postmortems record impact, mitigation, root causes and follow-up actions, and one that is never reviewed is wasted (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — incidents only · fetched 2026-10-14
- **Also good:** [How to write code review comments](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/comments.html) · Google Engineering Practices · comment on the code not the developer, explain why, label severity as nit, optional or FYI, and praise good work (*read*) · covers our angle: **partial** — the review comment that shows judgement, nothing on RFCs · fetched 2026-10-14
